# Slaughterbots activity — written alternative

## Context

This is a written alternative to the fictional *Slaughterbots* video used in F29AI Activity Sheet 1. It presents a speculative scenario for analysis, not a claim that all the capabilities described currently exist. Use the same questions on the activity sheet whether you read this account or watch the video.

## Scenario

A fictional technology company unveils a palm-sized autonomous aerial system at a public demonstration. The presenter claims that it can fly without a remote pilot, avoid obstacles, use onboard sensors, and identify a selected person from supplied data. A staged display presents these abilities as dependable. The company then describes groups navigating through streets, vehicles, windows, and buildings; sharing information; adapting routes; and continuing without continuous human direction.

The presentation quickly moves from one device to mass deployment. Large batches can supposedly be released with target descriptions rather than detailed instructions for every movement. Each unit combines stored or externally supplied information with its current observations to decide where to travel and what action to select. If deliberately equipped as a weapon, it could cause physical harm.

The scenario then shifts into fictional news reports. Autonomous units have allegedly been used against selected public officials, but investigators cannot determine whether a state, group, or individual authorised the attack. Reports later describe incidents at universities. Online activity and shared data appear to influence which people are selected while others nearby are ignored. The imagined result includes physical risk and uncertainty about who chose the targets and who is accountable.

For this system to work as portrayed, demanding assumptions must hold. Perception and identification must remain highly reliable in crowds and changing conditions. Localisation and navigation must work indoors and outdoors. Communication and group coordination must resist delay, interference, failure, and adversarial behaviour. The units need sufficient computation, energy, and control, and must reliably distinguish intended from unintended targets.

The scenario does not establish whether these requirements can be met at the claimed scale or reliability. It deliberately combines recognisable technologies with uncertain extrapolation to support an advocacy argument about autonomous weapons.
