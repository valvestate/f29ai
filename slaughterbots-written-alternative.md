# Slaughterbots activity — written alternative

## Context

This is a written alternative to the fictional *Slaughterbots* video used in F29AI Activity Sheet 1. It presents a speculative scenario for analysis, not a claim that all the capabilities described currently exist. Use the same questions on the activity sheet whether you read this account or watch the video.

## Scenario

In a fictional near-future setting, an organisation deploys large numbers of small autonomous aerial systems across a busy urban district. Each unit is designed to navigate streets, buildings, corridors, and crowded public spaces while avoiding obstacles and adapting its route when access points are blocked. Onboard cameras and other sensors allow a unit to search for, locate, and track people or objects as conditions change.

Before deployment, the systems receive stored information about intended targets. They may also receive updates from an external service while operating. Each unit combines this information with its sensor observations when selecting where to travel and what action to take. Units exchange limited status and location information, allowing them to divide search areas, coordinate movement, and continue operating when some members of the group fail or lose contact. They select many actions locally rather than waiting for continuous instructions from a human operator.

The scenario proposes that these systems could be manufactured and deployed in groups at considerable scale. If deliberately equipped and used as weapons, their physical actions could cause harm. Their operation is nevertheless dependent on demanding technical conditions. Perception and identification would need to remain highly reliable despite crowding, occlusion, changing light, and misleading inputs. Localisation and navigation would need to be accurate indoors and outdoors. Communication and coordination would need to tolerate delay, loss, interference, and adversarial behaviour. Each unit would need sufficient computation, energy, and physical control for the mission duration. Most importantly, the system would need to distinguish reliably between intended and unintended targets while circumstances and available information continued to change.

The account does not establish how reliably such a system could meet these conditions, how errors would propagate through a group, or which parts represent current capability rather than speculative extrapolation.
